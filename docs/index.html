01 · Valid only as tested

The small deviations are the ones that slip through.

A penetration seal is approved for the set-up that was fire-tested: construction, pipe material, diameter, wall thickness, insulation type, thickness and even product. Projects drift in small ways. Here is one of them, exactly as the app shows it.

Deviation

G-K-14 · Copper Ø22×1, Armaflex AF 13 mm

220 mm hollow-core deck · REI 60 · basement
Nearest tested: ETA-21/0110 A.4.8 · page 56
ETA-21/0110 · A.4.8 · p. 56

One line, with the evidence beside it

Same pipe, same diameter, same insulation thickness, same EI. The tested solution names Kaiflex ST; the project uses Armaflex. That is the deviation, and it is the only one. The adviser sees it in seconds instead of re-reading 56 pages.

And when one change would fix it, it says so

Copper hot water with 30 mm stone wool through the decks has no EI 60 row in this ETA (only E). The tested alternative: 9-13 mm elastomeric insulation with Multiwrap, A.4.8, EI 60.

Checked for every seal

construction · thickness · material · diameter (incl. the ETA's intermediate-size graphs) · wall thickness · insulation type, thickness and product · classification · pipe-end configuration (open/closed system) · hole size · edge distance · distance between seals · fill ratio

02 · The workflow

From the consultant's model to a seal you can document.

FindEvery service crossing an EI/REI wall or deck in the IFC: material, size, insulation, system, the architect's opening.
CheckAgainst 293 tested rows extracted from the ETA, 14 parameters, each with section and page.
ExplainDeviations named, nearest tested row, a tested alternative when one change would do.
DocumentExcel/PDF register with QR site labels, BCF issues, seals written into the IFC, assessment drafts.
AskA phone-enquiry checker in the browser and an MCP server for AI assistants, on the same rules.
